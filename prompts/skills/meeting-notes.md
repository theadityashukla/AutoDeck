# Meeting notes

Notes exist so that someone who was not in the room, or was and has forgotten, knows what
was decided, who is doing what by when, and what is still open. Everything else serves that.
A reader skims the Decisions and Action items first and trusts them completely, so those
sections carry the highest bar.

## What each section holds

- **Summary.** Two to four items: what the meeting was for and what came out of it. Written
  last. Each item is a sentence a manager could forward on its own. Not a list of topics
  ("Discussed timelines") but what was concluded about them ("The pilot moves to the first
  week of February, pending data access").
- **Attendees.** People the transcript shows were there: they speak and are named, are
  addressed, or name themselves. Someone only mentioned ("we should loop in Raj") was not
  there; if they matter, they belong in an action or a question. A name from the person's
  `--attendees` list that the transcript never shows stays out of the notes; mention it in
  your note to the person if it matters.
- **Themes.** The few subjects the meeting spent its time on, each with what was said about
  it in one or two sentences. Not a transcript summary in order: group by subject.
- **Decisions.** Only what was actually decided: someone proposed it and the room agreed,
  or someone with the authority said so. "We could move it to Feb" followed by talk of
  something else is not a decision; it is an open question. State the decision and, if the
  meeting gave one, the reason.
- **Key dates.** Every date or deadline said, with what it is for, the date **as said**, and
  its precision. "The sixteenth" is a day, and you do not add a month the meeting did not
  say. "First week of February" is a week. "End of Q1" is a quarter. "In two weeks" is
  relative and stays relative: nobody said what it is relative to on a calendar. Never turn
  a vaguer date into a sharper one.
- **Action items.** Each is something a person or team will do. An owner only when the
  transcript shows someone took it on or was given it, and they did not refuse. Otherwise
  no owner: it reads "Owner TBD", which is useful information, not a gap to fill. Due date
  only if said. Start with the verb: "Send the revised timeline to the client".
- **Open questions and risks.** What was raised and not settled, what someone was worried
  about, what is blocked on someone outside the room, and anything you could not make out
  (a name you cannot spell, a figure the transcript garbles). Each says what would settle it,
  if the meeting said.

An empty section is fine. "None noted" is truer than an item invented to fill it.

## Decided, planned, hoped, guessed

People in meetings speak in degrees. Keep the degree.

| They said | The note says |
|---|---|
| "Let's go with Databricks." (agreed) | Decided to use Databricks. |
| "We're aiming for the sixteenth." | The target is the sixteenth. |
| "I think the pilot slipped." | X thinks the pilot slipped (unconfirmed). |
| "Can we get data access by then?" | Open: whether data access is possible by then. |
| "I'll try to send it Friday." | Owner tries to send it Friday. |

A note that firms up what was said is the notes equivalent of a slide that overclaims its
source, and the validator treats it the same way.

## Quotes

Each item cites the turns that prove it, one quote per turn, copied exactly from the
transcript with its mistakes. The quote is the evidence; the item is your plain, correctly
spelled statement of it. Pick the words that carry the point: for a decision, the turn where
it was agreed, not the turn where it was first floated; for an action, the turn where the
owner took it on. When agreement comes in a later turn ("Yes, let's do that"), cite both
the proposal and the agreement.

## What to leave out

Small talk, technical trouble ("can you hear me?"), repetition, and anything said in passing
that nobody acted on. Personal remarks about people. Nothing goes in the notes that was not
said in this meeting: not background you know, not what the memory says unless the meeting
says it too.

## Before you finish

Read your Decisions and Action items as someone who will act on them tomorrow:
- Would everyone in the room agree each decision was made?
- Would each owner agree they took it on?
- Is every date as precise as it was said, and no more?
- Is anything that was decided or assigned missing?
- Is every name spelled as the engagement spells it, and every unsure spelling flagged as an
  open question?
