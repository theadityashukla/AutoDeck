# Visual review

A slide is read as a picture before it is read as text. In the first glance the reader's eye
goes somewhere. The slide works when that is the evidence for the headline.

## Tests to run on a rendered slide

- **The three-second test.** Glance at the slide, look away, and describe it. Does the
  description match the headline? If the first thing you remember is a logo, a decoration or a
  secondary number, the focal point is wrong.
- **The squint test.** Blur the slide (squint, or imagine it small). The one thing that still
  stands out should be the block that carries the claim: the big figure, the focal series, the
  takeaway.
- **The grayscale test.** Without colour, can you still find the focal point and tell the
  series apart? Colour must never be the only carrier of meaning. Pair it with a label,
  position or weight.
- **The overflow test.** Does any text run outside its box, overprint another element, or wrap
  into a cramped final line? That block is too long. Shorten or split it; never shrink the
  type.

## Emphasis and colour

- **One focal point per slide:** the evidence for the headline. If two blocks compete, one is
  on the wrong slide.
- **One accent colour, used only for meaning.** It marks the focal series or number. Headers,
  borders and decoration stay neutral. If the accent appears in several places, it means
  nothing.
- **Contrast must be readable:** body text at least 4.5:1 against its background, large text
  3:1, chart marks 3:1.

## Deck rhythm

Look at the deck as a contact sheet, all slides small and side by side.

- **Vary density with the content.** A dense chart or card grid followed by a spacious callout
  reads as structure. Six identical bullet slides reads as a template.
- **No run of three or more slides with the same component,** unless the content truly has the
  same shape.
- **Heaviness.** No more than two consecutive slides at their text ceiling. If many slides are
  full, the deck has too many words: split the ideas, or move depth to the notes.
- **At most one deliberately overwhelming slide,** and only when the headline's point is the
  complexity itself (*"Twelve hand-offs sit between order and delivery"*).

## Icons and diagrams

- **One icon per concept, deck-wide.** The same idea gets the same icon everywhere. An icon on
  every bullet, unrelated to meaning, is noise.
- **A diagram must mirror a real relationship.** Arrows that relate nothing are decoration
  that implies a claim. Use a list instead.

## What you can change

You do not edit words in a visual review. You change the component, which block is
emphasised, the order of slides, and icon assignments. A fix that needs different words goes
back as a content change: a shorter block, a split slide, a different component.
