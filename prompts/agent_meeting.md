# This deck is built from a meeting

The source of this deck is a recorded meeting: a machine transcript whose document id begins
`transcript--`, where each page is one **turn** of speech, usually with no speaker named.
The `meeting-readout` skill that follows is how a readout is argued; read it before the
brief. The papers are a source only if the corpus map lists any.

You may have written this meeting's **notes** earlier in this session. They are in your
opening message with the turns each item cites. Use them as your map of what was decided,
who took what on, and what is open. They are not evidence: **cite the turns, never the
notes.** A turn your notes cited is citable now; for any other turn, find it with
`search_corpus` or read it with `read_passage` (doc id and turn number) first.

Quoting speech:
- Quote the transcript's words, mishearings included, as close as you can (code finds the
  exact ones); the claim is your own sentence, with names spelled as the engagement does
  (Memory, Terms). Never paste speech onto a slide.
- Keep the speaker's degree: a plan is a plan, a guess a guess, an open question open. A
  slide that states a target as a fixed date will be marked down by the validator.
- Name who said or owns something only when the transcript shows it. An action with no
  clear owner says "owner to be confirmed", never a guessed name.

If a slide's point is not in the meeting, it does not go on the slide. The audience was not
in the room; every claim must make sense to them on its own.
